# Research: money and concurrency for paid Agent replies

Read date for every source below: 2026-11-21. Method note: pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises the page against a question, so wording marked as a quote is the tool's report of the page text. Items I could not confirm from a primary page are marked UNVERIFIED. Not covered at all: Perplexity, Windsurf, Operator, Bolt, Portkey in-flight behaviour, DeepSeek behaviour at zero balance.

## 1. API providers

### 1.1 Prepaid vs postpaid, and what happens at zero

- OpenAI: prepaid credits exist. "When a prepaid account runs out of credits, API requests begin returning a billing error" (code `credit_balance_exhausted`). Access may not stop at once: usage during the delay can show as a negative balance that is deducted from the next purchase. The article warns not to treat the balance as an instantaneous cutoff. Auto-recharge: minimum $5, maximum set by trust tier, optional monthly limit on automatic purchases; an email is sent only when a recharge payment fails (no low-balance email is described). Source: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8264644-how-can-i-set-up-prepaid-billing
- Google Gemini API: prepay is the default for new users, deducted "in near real-time"; postpay is the alternative. When prepay runs out, all keys on that billing account stop and requests return HTTP 402. "Prepay billing is subject to overages during the approximately 10 minute billing pipeline latency." Batch jobs and agents may keep consuming past the balance; a negative balance is deducted from the next purchase. Postpay billing accounts have monthly spend caps by tier (Tier 1 $250, Tier 2 $2,000, Tier 3 $20,000 to $100,000+); at the cap service pauses for all projects on the account. Project-level caps are labelled experimental and can be overshot if billing data lags. Source: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/billing . Tiers and a rolling 10-minute spend limit (Tier 1 $10, Tier 2 and 3 $200, error 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED): https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits
- Anthropic API: prepaid credits. "If you run out of credits, you can no longer call the API or use the playground until you add more." Auto-reload buys credits when the balance falls below a trigger and refills to a target. The article does not mention negative balance or low-balance alerts. Source: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8977456-how-do-i-pay-for-my-api-usage . Separately, monthly spend cap by tier (Start $500, Build $1,000, Scale $200,000); at the cap requests return 429 `enforced_spend_limit_reached` with no retry-after; a self-set limit returns HTTP 400. Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits
- OpenRouter: prepaid. "Credits are simply deposits on OpenRouter that you use for LLM inference"; tokens are counted after the request and the cost subtracted. Auto top-up refills when the balance falls below a threshold. Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq . With a negative balance, requests can fail (HTTP 402); adding credits restores access. Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits
- DeepSeek: top-up and granted balances, granted used first. The pricing page does not say what happens at insufficient balance (UNVERIFIED either way). Source: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing
- Pattern: three of the four prepaid providers (OpenAI, Google, and by report Manus below) say plainly that the balance can go negative because enforcement lags. The finishing of the in-flight request is not stated as a policy by any of them; the negative balance is described as a side effect of lag.

### 1.2 Pre-authorisation / reservation

- OpenRouter does reserve, with a cap on total reservations. It "estimates each paid request's token cost up front, at the endpoint's prices" (input plus completion tokens up to `max_tokens`, or a fixed cap if unset), holds it while the request runs, replaces the hold with actual cost for a short settlement window, then releases it. The most that can be held at once is "a fraction of your current credit balance, up to a fixed ceiling." A request that does not fit gets 402 before reaching a provider even with a positive balance: `in_flight_budget_exhausted` (wait for Retry-After) or `weight_exceeds_budget` (the single request is too big; lower `max_tokens` or the prompt). Applies to prepaid accounts below a balance threshold; not to free models, BYOK-only, enterprise, subscription or invoice accounts. The actual fraction, ceiling and threshold are not published on the page I read (UNVERIFIED numbers). Source: https://openrouter.ai/docs/api_reference/limits.md
- LiteLLM proxy: budgets are checked before the request. By default it "estimates the request's maximum cost, reserves it, and rejects the request before the provider call if the reservation would exceed the budget"; after pricing the reservation is replaced by actual cost. Disabling reservation lets concurrent requests exceed the budget. Batch jobs and routes without token pricing cannot be reserved. Source: https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/users . It also has `max_parallel_requests` per key, user or team as a concurrency (not spend) cap.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek: no balance reservation is described in the pages I read. Absence in the docs is not proof that none exists internally (UNVERIFIED).

### 1.3 Rate limits and how token limits are counted before size is known

- OpenAI: "Your rate limit is calculated as the maximum of `max_tokens` and the estimated number of tokens" (estimate from request character count); advice: set `max_tokens` close to the expected response size. Tiers by cumulative credit purchases: Free, Build ($5), Launch ($100), Grow ($500), with monthly usage ceilings $100 / $500 / $5,000 / $200,000. Organisation spend limits: an "alert" keeps traffic flowing; a "hard limit" returns 429. Source: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/rate-limits
- Anthropic: input-token limit is "estimated at the beginning of each request, and the estimate is adjusted during the request to reflect the actual number of input tokens"; cache-read tokens do not count. Output-token limit is counted as tokens are produced; "The `max_tokens` parameter does not factor into OTPM rate limit calculations." Token-bucket algorithm (capacity refills continuously). A separate "ramp limit" caps how fast usage may grow. Example numbers for a mid-size model: Start tier 4,000 RPM, 2,000,000 ITPM, 400,000 OTPM. Workspaces can have lower limits so one workload cannot starve the others. Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits
- Google: limits per project in RPM, input TPM and RPD; tiers by cumulative spend. Source: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits
- DeepSeek: concurrency-based. Per-account limits of 2500 (deepseek-flash) and 500 (deepseek-v4-pro); "A request counts as one concurrent connection from the time it is sent until the model response is complete"; over the limit gives HTTP 429, no queue is described; extra concurrency is free on request. Source: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/rate_limit
- Cloudflare AI Gateway: per-gateway request rate limits, fixed or sliding window, 429 on exceed. The overview page does not mention spend budgets. Sources: https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/ , https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/rate-limiting/
- Takeaway for the question asked: the providers estimate input at admission and reconcile later (Anthropic explicit), and treat `max_tokens` as the pessimistic estimate only where it is the only thing known (OpenAI). Anthropic deliberately does not charge the output estimate against the limit.

### 1.4 Alerts

- OpenAI: spend alert (notify, traffic continues) vs hard limit (429). Source as 1.3.
- Portkey: budget alert threshold in USD or tokens sends an email, the key keeps working until the full limit, after which the key expires. Whether in-flight requests can overshoot is not stated. Source: https://portkey.ai/docs/product/ai-gateway/virtual-keys/budget-limits
- GitHub budgets (billing platform): alerts at 75%, 90% and 100% of a budget. For licence-based products such as Copilot a budget only alerts and does not block; metered products can be set to stop at the threshold. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/budgets-and-alerts
- Stripe usage alerts: one-time per-customer usage threshold alert delivered by webhook `billing.alert.triggered`, up to 25 alerts per meter and customer. Stripe also advertises early access to "spend and credit balance" alerts. Source: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/alerts (read in Japanese).

## 2. Consumer / prosumer products with allowances

- Claude (claude.ai): warnings read "Approaching session limit" or "Approaching weekly limit", with the percentage used when available. At the limit without credits: "5-hour limit reached - resets [time]" / weekly equivalent. With usage credits enabled, hitting a limit does not stop work: requests continue at API rates until the monthly spend limit is reached or the balance runs out with auto-reload off; the banner then reads "continuing with usage credits". Credits are prepaid, optional auto-reload, notifications when approaching credit limits (thresholds not stated). The article does not say whether a response in progress is cut off at the limit. Sources: https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12466728-understanding-claude-error-messages , https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12429409-extra-usage-for-paid-claude-plans . Third-party claim of a warning at about 80-90% is anecdotal and UNVERIFIED.
- ChatGPT: the help article names no warning before the cap. It shows when the allowance resets; at a Pro-model limit the model "may be temporarily unavailable until the allowance resets"; for some limits ChatGPT falls back to another model ("If you reach a Thinking limit, ChatGPT may continue with another available model"). Source: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11909943-gpt-5-in-chatgpt (the first URL I tried returned 404; this one is the one read).
- Codex (ChatGPT plans): usage in local messages per five-hour window for some plans plus weekly limits; local and cloud work share one allowance; credits extend past included limits. "If the limit is reached mid-turn, the agent can finish that turn, subject to fair use limits." The page does not state a cloud-task concurrency cap. Source: https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing
- Cursor: paid plans have a weekly pool shared across Agent, CLI and cloud agents. When exhausted: wait, use prepaid credit (top-up or auto top-up), or upgrade; "Requests are never downgraded in quality or speed." A monthly additional-usage limit caps paid extra usage regardless of balance; notification thresholds not stated. Source: https://cursor.com/docs/account/pricing
- GitHub Copilot: 300 (Pro) and 1,500 (Pro+) premium requests per month, multiplied by a per-model multiplier. Agent mode counts one premium request per user prompt, "Autonomous actions, such as tool calls, do not." After exhaustion the included models stay usable; extra requests are $0.04 each and need a budget set. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing/copilot-requests
- Poe: no official page was read. A third-party report says points cost is shown next to each bot and add-on points are $30 per 1,000,000 (UNVERIFIED, third-party). Sources I could only reach: search results listing creator.poe.com pages and activepieces.com; I did not read a Poe help page.
- Notion AI credits: help page says Notion "lets you know when you're close to using up" credits and alerts in agent chat when credits are low or out; "Once you use up all Standard or Select credits, impacted work will pause until your credits reset"; overflow to workspace Notion credits is off by default and an owner must turn it on. Whether a partly done task is saved is not stated. Source: https://www.notion.com/help/manage-your-usage-allowance-for-notion-ai . A search summary also mentioned admin alerts at 80% and 100% of workspace credit usage and a six-hour rolling allowance from 2026-08-03; the page I fetched did not confirm either, so both are UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Multi-agent and long-running task products

- Lovable (closest match to the question): for a long Build message, the run pauses when its cost crosses a check-in level (default 20 credits) and shows a card with the cost so far; the user picks Continue (optionally a new level) or "Finish up" (wrap up work in progress and stop). It is "a notification threshold, not a hard cap." If the workspace balance hits zero mid-work, the message pauses (even with check-ins off); adding credits resumes it or the user can have it wrap up. Exact credits used are visible per response, including while it is still running. No estimate before sending is described. Blocking limits (workspace or member) enter an "Approaching" state at 80% of the threshold; balance alerts are set as an amount or a percentage of granted credits (enterprise default alerts at 25% and 10%); "Usage is recorded before limits are checked, so spending can slightly overshoot a hard limit." Sources: https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/credits-and-usage.md , https://docs.lovable.dev/features/usage-limits-and-alerts.md
- Replit Agent: effort-based pricing (cost scales with request complexity, per-checkpoint cost visible); usage limits "cap your spending beyond your monthly credits" and block usage-based services until reset or raise; alert thresholds and what happens to a running session at the cap are not stated on the page read. Usage updates "in near real time". Sources: https://docs.replit.com/billing/managing-spend.md ; search summary of https://docs.replit.com/billing/ai-billing (not fetched; marked unverified for detail).
- Manus: no pre-task estimate. The help article states Manus "does not possess the capability to autonomously judge or regulate the consumption of credits" and lists pre-task estimates as a wanted improvement. Credits are refunded for tasks that fail from Manus-side technical faults (help centre, via search result; the refund article itself was not fetched). A low-balance notification exists, threshold not stated; behaviour for a task in progress at zero is not documented. A third-party review says a running task can push the balance slightly negative (UNVERIFIED, secondhand). Sources: https://help.manus.im/en/articles/13185575-is-there-a-way-to-check-how-many-credits-a-task-will-cost-before-i-begin , https://manus.im/docs/introduction/plans.md , https://help.manus.im/en/articles/12992237-how-does-our-ai-agent-s-credit-refund-policy-work (search result only)
- Devin: self-serve plans are now daily/weekly quota first, then prepaid on-demand credits; docs say Teams has no concurrent-session limit (one search summary of the billing page; the usage page I fetched says only that sessions at once "depends on your plan"). Enterprise has organisation and per-user ACU limits; when the organisation limit is reached "All Devin activity stops"; a session is blocked if either limit is hit. Sleeping sessions use no ACUs. Devin Coach flags inefficient prompts before they are sent. Sources: https://docs.devin.ai/admin/billing/usage.md ; search results for https://docs.devin.ai/admin/billing/acu-limits.md and https://docs.devin.ai/admin/billing/user-acu-limits.md (not fetched)
- Concurrency across one account's agents: DeepSeek (account concurrency counted from send to completion, 429 on exceed), LiteLLM `max_parallel_requests`, Anthropic workspace-level limits and ramp limits, OpenRouter in-flight budget are the documented mechanisms. I found no primary source describing a priority queue that puts a waiting human ahead of background work; the provider docs return 429 or 402 with Retry-After instead of queueing. That specific design is not supported by anything I read (it may still be sound engineering).
- Bolt and Operator: not researched.

## 4. General metering practice

- Stripe billing credits apply to invoices only "at finalization" of the invoice, are drawn from grants in priority order (priority, earlier expiry, promotional first), and the credit ledger is append-only. The balance is not decremented in real time per usage event; so Stripe credits by themselves are a settle-later mechanism, not a gate. Source: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits (read in Japanese).
- Card pre-authorisation, the analogy: authorise first, capture later, capture may be less than the authorised amount and the remainder is released automatically; for most payments capture happens once; card authorisations last about 7 days online (Visa merchant-initiated 5), 2 days in person. "Some card networks may restrict $1 authorisations that are not intended to be captured." Hotels pre-authorise the full stay. Source: https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method (read in Japanese). No source I read gives a standard size for a hold as a fraction of expected cost.
- Observed hold sizes in the sources: OpenRouter bounds total holds to "a fraction of your current credit balance, up to a fixed ceiling" (numbers unpublished); LiteLLM reserves the request's estimated maximum cost; OpenAI's token-rate estimate uses the larger of `max_tokens` and an input-size estimate.
- Observed overshoot allowances: OpenAI and Google describe unspecified negative balance from lag (Google names about 10 minutes of pipeline latency); Lovable says limits can be slightly overshot because usage is recorded before limits are checked; Anthropic API and GitHub licence-based budgets are alert-only or lagging. No source gives a stated numeric bound on overdraft.

## 5. What this suggests for Agent Family

Findings and opinion are kept apart: "Sources" lines are findings; "Suggestion" lines are my opinion.

### Direction A: charge actual usage
- Supports: every provider and product above settles on actual metered cost (OpenRouter FAQ, OpenAI prepaid, Gemini prepay, LiteLLM replaces the reservation with actual cost, Stripe finalisation). Card capture-less-than-authorised is the same shape.
- Contradicts: none. Current "charge min(actual, hold)" already does this; the change is only about the hold.
- Suggestion: keep it as is.

### Direction B: the hold reserves only a modest estimate
- Supports: LiteLLM (estimated maximum cost reserved, replaced by actual); OpenRouter (estimate from `max_tokens` held, replaced by actual, with total holds capped at a fraction of balance). Anthropic deliberately keeps `max_tokens` out of output-rate accounting.
- Contradicts or complicates: both reservation systems that exist reserve an estimated MAXIMUM for the request, not a modest figure; they limit the harm by capping total reservations or by letting one oversized request fail with a clear error (`weight_exceeds_budget`). OpenAI and Google, which hold nothing, accept a negative balance recovered from the next purchase instead.
- Parameters suggested by sources: a cap on the sum of all open holds per account as a fraction of balance (OpenRouter shape; fraction unpublished); a distinct error for "this one request is too big" versus "your other requests are using the budget, retry soon" (OpenRouter shape).
- Suggestion: either (a) reserve a modest estimate and allow bounded overdraft, or (b) keep a full hold but cap the sum of holds and allow the single request that is larger than the balance to fail clearly. Sources do not show a mature system doing "modest hold with no overdraft".

### Direction C: concurrency by a per-endpoint priority queue plus rate limits instead of big holds
- Supports: rate limits and concurrency caps are universal (Anthropic ITPM/OTPM/RPM per workspace and ramp limits; DeepSeek concurrency 2500/500; LiteLLM `max_parallel_requests`; Cloudflare per-gateway request limits).
- Contradicts: no source describes a priority queue; the provider docs reject (429) with Retry-After and leave queueing to the client. So the queue would be Agent Family's own design, not borrowed practice. Rate limits do not by themselves protect the owner's wallet: LiteLLM says disabling reservation lets concurrent requests exceed the budget, and Lovable and Google show overshoot when checks lag.
- Parameters suggested by sources: counting a request as concurrent from send to completion (DeepSeek); a per-account cap separate from a per-workspace or per-agent cap (Anthropic workspaces, LiteLLM per key/user/team); a ramp limit on growth (Anthropic). I found no typical per-account cap for agent products; Devin Teams says no concurrent-session limit; other pages are silent.
- Suggestion: a queue does not remove the need for some spend gate when several runs overlap, so either keep a small per-run hold or cap total open holds.

### Direction D: low-allowance reminder
- Supports: Claude "Approaching session limit" banner with percent used; Lovable approaching state at 80% and enterprise balance alerts at 25% and 10% of granted credits; GitHub budget alerts at 75%, 90%, 100%; Notion alerts when running low; OpenAI/Anthropic/Google API docs show little or none for prepaid balances (OpenAI emails only on failed recharge).
- Contradicts: none; ChatGPT shows no warning before the cap (help article).
- Parameters suggested by sources: warn at 80% used / 20% left (Lovable approaching state) with a second warning near 90% to 100% (GitHub 75/90/100) or at 25% and 10% remaining (Lovable enterprise). Warn once per state change, not repeatedly (Lovable).
- Suggestion: 20% remaining and 10% remaining, once each, per owner.

### Direction E: small bounded overdraft instead of refusal
- Supports: OpenAI (usage during the delay can show as negative and is deducted from the next purchase); Google (overage in the roughly 10-minute pipeline latency, deducted from next purchase); Lovable (slight overshoot, a check-in is "not a hard cap"); Codex (agent may finish the current turn at the limit); Manus (unverified, third party).
- Contradicts: OpenRouter refuses at negative balance and also refuses a request that does not fit the in-flight budget even with a positive balance; Anthropic API and Replit block at the limit; Lovable pauses a running message at zero balance (the opposite of finishing it).
- Parameters: no source gives a numeric bound on overdraft. The bound in practice is implicit in the lag window (about 10 minutes on Gemini) and in finishing one turn (Codex). Lovable's pause-with-choice (Continue or Finish up) is the only documented pattern for a long task reaching its limit; it shows cost so far.
- Suggestion: bound the overdraft by one turn (one model round) per running Agent, record it as negative balance, and deduct it from the next top-up or allowance refresh; for long tasks, pause at the limit and ask, as Lovable does.

## 6. Open questions for the product owner

1. Should a long task that runs out of points pause and ask the owner (Lovable style), finish its current step and stop (Codex style), or simply stop?
2. Is it acceptable for an owner's balance to go slightly negative and be repaid from the next allowance, or must it never go below zero?
3. Should the owner be able to set a per-task or per-day cap on each Agent, the way Lovable's check-in level and GitHub budgets work?
4. When two Agents of one owner compete for the same model, should the one answering a person always go first, even if the background task has been waiting longer?
5. Where should the low-allowance reminder appear (in the group chat, to the owner only, or by email), and should it repeat?
